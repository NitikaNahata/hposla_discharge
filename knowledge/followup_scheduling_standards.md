# Follow-Up Scheduling Standards

SYNTHETIC reference generated for this capstone. Not a clinical guideline.

## Booking before the patient leaves

A follow-up appointment arranged before the patient leaves the ward is attended substantially more
often than one the patient is asked to arrange themselves. Wherever the appointment can be booked
during the admission, it should be, and the confirmed date and time should appear in the discharge
packet rather than an instruction to telephone the clinic.

## Interval selection

The follow-up interval is driven by three factors and should be set by the shortest of them: the
condition's standard interval, the readmission-risk tier, and any medication change requiring
monitoring. A stable regimen on a low-risk patient takes the standard interval. A new
anticoagulant, a diuretic increase, or a newly started mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist all
compress the interval regardless of the underlying condition, because the monitoring requirement
is what governs.

## High-risk enhanced pathway

Patients in the high readmission-risk tier warrant an enhanced follow-up pathway: the primary
appointment within 7 days rather than the standard interval, a telephone check at 48 to 72 hours,
and consideration of home health nursing. The telephone check is the highest-yield element,
because it catches medication confusion and early symptom recurrence in the window where
intervention still prevents readmission.

## Transport

Transport is a routine cause of missed first appointments and is rarely assessed. Any patient with
limited mobility, no caregiver, or no means of driving should have transport arranged as part of
the follow-up plan rather than assumed. A patient who has previously missed appointments because
of transport will miss the next one for the same reason unless something changes.

## Ownership of pending results

Every pending investigation at discharge needs a named owner and a review date. Results that
belong to nobody are not reviewed.
