# Anticoagulation Discharge Guidance

SYNTHETIC reference generated for this capstone. Not a clinical guideline.

## Warfarin interactions at discharge

Warfarin is the medication most often implicated in early post-discharge adverse drug events,
because its effect is modified by a large number of commonly co-prescribed drugs and because the
onset of an interaction is frequently delayed past the point of discharge.

Fluconazole is a potent inhibitor of CYP2C9, the principal enzyme clearing S-warfarin. INR can
rise sharply within 2 to 3 days of starting it, with a substantial bleeding risk. This
combination should be avoided where an alternative antifungal exists. Where it is unavoidable,
the warfarin dose must be reduced pre-emptively and INR rechecked within 3 days under
anticoagulation-clinic supervision.

Amiodarone inhibits warfarin metabolism and has a half-life measured in weeks, so its effect
accumulates well after discharge. Anticipate a warfarin dose reduction of roughly one third.
Recheck INR within 3 to 5 days and then weekly for the first month.

Broad-spectrum antibiotics potentiate warfarin by reducing gut flora synthesis of vitamin K.
Check INR mid-course and again at completion.

## When a new interacting medication is started

Any patient discharged on warfarin with a newly started interacting medication requires an INR
check within 3 days, not at the routine interval. This is the single most important scheduling
decision in this group, and missing it is the dominant cause of early readmission among
anticoagulated patients.

## Direct oral anticoagulants

Apixaban is a substrate of both CYP3A4 and P-glycoprotein. Strong inhibitors of both, including
fluconazole, raise apixaban exposure and bleeding risk. NSAIDs should be avoided with any
anticoagulant because of additive bleeding risk from impaired platelet function and gastric
mucosal injury.

## Patient counselling

Take the anticoagulant at the same time each day and never double a missed dose. Report bleeding
that will not stop, blood in the urine or stool, a severe headache, or any head injury. Tell
every clinician and dentist about the anticoagulant before any procedure. Patients on warfarin
should keep vitamin K intake steady rather than avoiding leafy vegetables entirely.
