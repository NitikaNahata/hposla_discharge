# Community-Acquired Pneumonia Discharge Protocol

SYNTHETIC reference generated for this capstone. Not a clinical guideline.

## Pre-discharge requirements

A patient with community-acquired pneumonia should be afebrile for at least 24 hours before
discharge, with oxygen saturation at or above 92 percent on room air both at rest and on
ambulation. Ambulatory saturation matters more than resting saturation and is frequently omitted.
The patient must be tolerating oral intake and oral antibiotics, and the antibiotic course must be
defined with an explicit end date rather than left open.

## Follow-up intervals

Standard risk: primary care within 7 to 14 days.
Higher risk, including patients over 65 or with significant comorbidity: within 7 days.

A follow-up chest radiograph at 6 weeks is required to confirm radiographic resolution. This is
the single most commonly omitted element of pneumonia discharge planning, and it matters because a
consolidation that has not cleared at 6 weeks may indicate an underlying obstructing lesion. The
radiograph should be booked at discharge rather than left to the follow-up visit to arrange.

## Pending results

Blood cultures drawn on admission are frequently still pending at discharge. Any pending
microbiology must be listed explicitly in the discharge summary with a named clinician responsible
for reviewing the final result, since a positive culture reported after discharge otherwise has no
owner.

## Education priorities

Complete the entire antibiotic course even after feeling well. Expect fatigue and a residual cough
to persist for several weeks; this is normal recovery and not treatment failure. Return promptly if
symptoms worsen after an initial period of improvement, which suggests a secondary complication.

## Red-flag symptoms

Fever returning after it had settled. Increasing breathlessness, or breathlessness at rest. Chest
pain on breathing in. Coughing up blood. Confusion or unusual drowsiness, which in an older patient
may be the only sign of deterioration.

## Antibiotic interactions at discharge

Broad-spectrum antibiotics including amoxicillin-clavulanate potentiate warfarin by reducing gut
flora vitamin K synthesis. Any anticoagulated patient discharged on an antibiotic course needs an
INR check mid-course and again at completion. Patients with a documented penicillin allergy must
not receive amoxicillin-clavulanate; this cross-check is a required step, not a matter of judgement.
