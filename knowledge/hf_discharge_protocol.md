# Heart Failure Discharge Protocol

SYNTHETIC reference generated for this capstone. Not a clinical guideline.

## Pre-discharge requirements

A patient with acute decompensated heart failure should not be discharged until they are at or
near their documented dry weight and have been stable on an oral diuretic regimen for at least
24 hours. A basic metabolic panel within 24 hours of discharge must show stable potassium and
creatinine. Guideline-directed medical therapy should be reviewed and uptitrated wherever blood
pressure and renal function allow, since the post-discharge window is when uptitration is most
often missed.

## Follow-up intervals

Standard risk: cardiology or heart-failure clinic within 7 days of discharge.
High readmission risk: within 3 to 5 days, with an additional telephone check at 48 to 72 hours.
A basic metabolic panel is required within 5 to 7 days of any diuretic dose change or any change
to a renin-angiotensin system agent, and within 5 days of starting a mineralocorticoid receptor
antagonist such as spironolactone.

## Weight monitoring

Daily weight monitoring is the single most effective self-management intervention in heart
failure. The patient should weigh at the same time each morning, after urinating and before
eating, and record the reading. A gain of more than 2 kg (about 4 lb) over 2 days, or 2.5 kg over
a week, should trigger a call to the clinic. Patients discharged without a working set of scales
at home should have them supplied before leaving the ward.

## Red-flag symptoms

Weight gain over 2 kg in 2 days. Breathlessness at rest, or needing additional pillows to sleep.
New or worsening swelling of the ankles, legs or abdomen. Light-headedness or fainting. A heart
rate persistently below 50 or above 120. Chest pain.

## Readmission risk

Heart failure carries one of the highest 30-day readmission rates of any medical condition. The
two interventions with the strongest evidence are early follow-up within 7 days and reliable
daily weight monitoring. Patients who live alone, who have had a prior admission within 12
months, or who cannot reliably state their own diuretic dose at teach-back are at markedly
elevated risk and warrant an enhanced follow-up pathway.
